---
title: "Transitioning from Turkish Keyboard to English Keyboard"
date: 2026-05-25
---

TBH this whole journey started with the realisation that I do not code enough. LLMs code for us, we develop features at unprecented rate and we have a lot of reviewing to do. I guess... I wanted to code so bad... And I can't code at the speed that I wanted. It was due to my limited knowledge about the libraries and the technology that I've been using. Says not a single developer. So I decided to blame my Turkish keyboard for it.

Turkish keyboard shares all the letters that English keyboard (e.g. QWERTY) has. However it includes additional 6 letters `ö ş ğ ı ç ü`. As a result, the characters at the given order, `, ; [ ' . ]`, are put into places where I am only allowed to use with Shift ⇧ button pressed. These are the characters that are regularly used in coding. Hence it becomes very inconvenient to code sometimes and I've found myself copy pasting characters time to time.

The other disadvantage was to using IDE shortcurts. IDEs provide default shortcuts but they rarely work with Turkish layout. I usually had a spider sense what that shortcut might translate into Turkish layout. If that sense was not accurate, I was typing all alternatives manually to discover. Basically brute-forcing my way into a shortcut.

Perhaps another issue is how unhappy my colleague gets when he can't write something with my keyboard during pair programming. It is quite funny to see their reaction, but honestly I try my best to be a great teammate hence I change my layout immediately before they even start typing. Pair programming is very fun and productive thing to do and Turkish keyboard was making the life of my pairs a bit difficult.

You might have guessed that I moved to English QWERTY layout. It is true indeed. It took place a week ago and it was a painful thing to do. Especially at work, I had to be so patient with the speed of typing. I practiced at [https://www.keybr.com](https://www.keybr.com) daily for about 30 mins, perhaps at least that amount. Whenever I got bored, I opened it and started typing. This morning I noticed that I finished the journey there.

As of 2026-05-25, I type at speed 53 WPM and with 90% accuracy according to [https://monkeytype.com](https://monkeytype.com). I have difficulties still with the punctuation marks and those kind of things. I had to re-learn the IDE shortcuts as well. IDK the journey ahead. I deeply respect the people who are good at their craft, for the sake of craft. I wanna be just like them.
